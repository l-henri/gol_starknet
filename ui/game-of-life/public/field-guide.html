01 · the spirit

Why this exists

In the 1970s John Conway showed that four simple rules on a pixel grid produce endless, unpredictable life-like behaviour. But every Game of Life dies when you close the laptop — the creatures depend on their creator. A blockchain is an always-on computer that belongs to no one. Put the creatures there, give the world a button to push them forward, and they stop depending on anyone.

liberation

Set them free

A creature isn't "minted", it's set free: proven alive by the chain itself, then left to exist independently of its discoverer — like Theo Jansen's wind-powered Strandbeests, released onto a beach that never closes.

coordination

Kept alive by strangers

Nothing moves unless a human pays a little gas to advance it. The economy is built so that caring for existing creatures is the only way to earn the right to create new ones — interest in the piece literally becomes life for the garden.

the medium

The gas is the art

This is not a financial project. There is nothing to speculate on. Every fee burned feeding a creature is a brushstroke; the ledger of who kept what alive, for how long, is the artwork.

"If my creature is alive, it will live forever — as long as someone keeps pushing. I want to try and set them free." — from the project's purpose statement
02 · the creatures

Two species, one grid

Every drawing eventually meets its destiny: it either settles into a loop (a cycle of states that repeats forever) or it is a journey into one. Those two fates are the two NFT collections. A creature's identity is its entire symmetry family — the same shape shifted, rotated, or mirrored is the same creature, and the chain refuses to mint it twice.

BACT · "Digital Bacteria"

The living — loops

Patterns already inside their eternal cycle. They are the garden's residents: they breathe, age, and can be kept alive forever.

  • Period — how many states the cycle holds (rarer when long)
  • Age — generations actually lived; grows only when fed
  • Owner-tunable appearance (colours, speed) — the render is fully on-chain
WNDR · "Digital Wanderers"

The travellers — wanderers

Patterns still on their way: a moment of travel, caught. A wanderer is a frozen snapshot — it can't be fed — and its rarity is the length of its journey.

  • Journey — generations before it reaches its loop
  • Fate — alive (joins a moving loop) · frozen (a still life) · gone out (fades to nothing)
  • Finding a longer journey backwards is genuinely hard — that's the treasure hunt
Why copies can't exist. On a wrapping grid there are 13,448 ways to shift/rotate/mirror any pattern. The token's identity is computed from the whole family at once, so a copy of an existing creature produces the same token id — and the mint simply fails. One artwork, one creature, enforced by arithmetic rather than moderation.

The six verbs

Everything a visitor does maps to one of six actions. Only two cost anything beyond gas — and one of those earns the currency for the other.

Draw & discover

Sketch a seed on the grid and watch its destiny unfold on a slot-machine score: sequence, loop, amplitude. Finding a long loop feels like hitting a jackpot — because it is one.

Advance any creature — yours or a stranger's — by up to ~80 generations per signature. This is the piece's heartbeat and the only source of $NUT.

Adopt & pet

A pet is one ceremonial breath that also ties a bond: your name on the creature's caretaker list, with a 7-day clock. Neglect it and the bond wilts — the reaper passes. Bonds are transferable: hand one to a friend before a holiday, clock and all.

costs gas onlyearns 1 $NUT + the bond
5 /incubator

Keep

Bookmark discoveries you're not ready to set free, and resume long births mid-sequence. The incubator is the shelf of things not yet born.

costs nothingyields a saved destiny
6 /leaderboards

Compete

Five boards, one per way of caring: longest loops, methuselahs (longest life from the smallest seed), top breathers, caretakers, and the weekly discoveries — winnable by a newcomer.

costs nothingyields glory
04 · the loop

How it all feeds itself

The economy is a single circle: the only way to earn $NUT is to keep existing creatures alive, and the only use for $NUT is to set new ones free — who then need keeping alive. Demand for creation becomes life for everything already living. $NUT spent on births is escrowed, never recycled: a quiet counterweight to the faucet.

05 · why people play

Four ways to fall in

the high-score hunter

"I found a longer one."

The /create page is deliberately a slot machine: draw, spin, jackpot. Long loops and long journeys are provably rare, the leaderboards make rank public, and the weekly board resets so a newcomer can win. The project's most devoted user to date is eight years old.

the caretaker

"Three days before it wilts."

Pet bonds are tamagotchi mechanics with real stakes: a visible clock, a garden of wards, and loss if you drift away. Care is recurring, ritual, and recorded forever.

the collector

"There is exactly one."

Uniqueness is enforced by math, not curation — no copies, ever. The render is 100% on-chain (you own the generator, not a link), owner-tunable, and the creature's age is its provenance: a record of everyone who ever kept it alive.

the mathematician

"A census of a tiny universe."

A 41×41 torus is an unexplored space: which loops exist? What's the longest journey? Methuselah-hunting (maximum life from minimum seed) is a genuine open search problem with a public scoreboard.

What is valuable here? Length (long periods and long journeys are hard to find) · age (care is cumulative and unfakeable) · primacy (first of a family, forever) · and permanence (nothing depends on a server, a company, or the artist).

identity

One family, one token

A creature's token id is the hash of its family's canonical form. The chain can't search 13,448 symmetries itself, so the minter verifies a supplied witness instead — and dishonest identities are permanently burnable by anyone, for a bounty.

the janitors

Bots do the grim work

Reaping wilted bonds, burning farmed copies and dishonest ids — all permissionless, all bounty-paying, all deliberately absent from the website. Humans only ever see the warning: "le faucheur passe."
